Blind Lens

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

The instrument is intact; part of what it should see is missing. Thick white bezel and handle, deep-navy glass, and a straight chord cut across the field of view with everything past it dead red.

The glass is the brand gradient darkened to 42% rather than left as tile colour — that gives the disc mass, so the circle survives at 16px instead of depending on a hairline. The bezel stays closed on purpose: the blindness is inside the field of view, not a bite out of the object.

Honest caveat: the bezel loses even weight where red meets white, and the handle merges with the ring into one white form at 16px.

Rejected: a centre-apex pie wedge (read as an analytics chart); letting the wedge eat through the bezel (red bled into the violet and the circle became a blob).

Half Blind Bot

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

It is looking, and only half-registering. A rounded crawler head with two identical eyes — same shape, size and baseline — one pure white, one an extinguished socket.

This one was decided by measurement rather than taste. Solid #ef4444 on the gradient body is only 1.5:1 luminance contrast — it separates by hue alone, which antialiasing destroys at 16px and colour-blind users never receive at all. So the dead eye is near-black burnt red #2b0510 at 3.2:1: full contrast at 16px, still visibly warm at 48 and 128. The lit eye measures 5.7:1.

Strongest at 16px of the four — three shapes, maximum contrast, no fine detail to lose.

Rejected: a visor slit (read unmistakably as a UI toggle switch); circular eyes (soft edges); a red core inside the dark socket (maroon sludge); a shut-eye dash (cute, and a hairline).

Redacted

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

Your content exists; the machine is not allowed to have it. Three brutally heavy struck-out bars run off the left edge in a descending rag — blacked-out lines of text — and from the last one a thin tail escapes past the ink to the full measure of the first line: the one sliver the crawler still gets. A small paper chip is punched through the top bar, one fragment that was not covered properly.

No tile, no plate, no gradient. The bars bleed off the left edge so the mark is a crop of a larger struck-out mass rather than an object sitting on a background. The redaction is the mark, not an annotation on a drawn document.

Colour was decided by measurement, not taste. Redaction wants to be black, and black is unusable: on Chrome's dark toolbar #292a2d a near-black bar measures ~1.3:1 and the icon simply is not there. The luminance band that clears 3:1 against both #f1f3f4 and #292a2d is narrow — relative luminance 0.17–0.28 — and only one convincing redaction colour lives inside it: the red of a classified stamp. #E8412F measures 3.61:1 light and 3.57:1 dark, deliberately balanced rather than optimised for either. That band is too narrow to hold a second ink, so the mark is one colour.

The paper chip #FFF7EC would disappear on a light toolbar if it ever touched it, so it never does — it is inset with a full grid unit of ink on all four sides. Its contrast is against the ink (3.78:1), which is identical in both themes.

16px: the 16px file contains zero partially-covered pixels — two opaque colours, every edge on a whole pixel. The rag, the escaping tail and the chip all survive. What is lost is nuance: at 16px the chip is 3×2px and reads as a notch rather than as a fragment of a word.

Honest caveat: heavy left-aligned bars of descending length sit near the align-left / list glyph. The weight is what separates them — these are 4/16 of the canvas thick, where a text glyph is nearer 1.5/16 — but the redaction reading is helped by knowing the product.

Rejected: two equal full-bleed bars (read as a flag, no ragged rhythm); near-black ink (dies on the dark toolbar — the whole reason for the red); amber or paper for the surviving fragment (1.4:1 and 1.05:1 on a light toolbar, so the punchline vanished exactly where it mattered); a full-height slot cut through a bar (reads as a gap on light and a glow on dark — two different marks); a second red for tonal depth (nothing else fits the 0.17–0.28 band).

Two Layers

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

The page you see and the page the crawler gets, offset. A solid white slab pushed down-left, and behind it a hollow plane outlined in red with a darkened void inside.

Offset is 5/16 of plane width — enough to stack, not enough to hide the rear. The layers are separated by a 1px knockout ring in the gradient so they never merge into one blob, which is the failure mode for overlapping shapes at 16px.

Honest caveat: it is the most abstract of the four and sits closest to a generic “duplicate / copy” icon. It says two things differ; it does not say crawler or blindness on its own.

Rejected: a white rear outline (read as duplicate); a 2px stroke (swallowed the void).

Vanishing Page

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

A page that stops existing partway through. Two chunky content bars on an indigo→violet page that ends in a hard red edge, with nothing below it.

Two bars, not three — three turned to mud. Every edge snapped to the 1/16 grid so 16px lands on whole pixels instead of blurring.

Honest caveat, and it is the real one: at 16px this reads as “blue box with a red footer” before it reads as “page that stops”. Empty margin in an icon is invisible as a design element — the viewer sees absence of icon, not absence of content. The void only does its work once you already know what you are looking at.

Rejected: a red line overhanging both sides (read instantly as a laptop on a stand); three bars; non-grid proportions.
