Round two: no tile, no gradient, no stock glyph

The first round came back as one icon with four different stickers — a rounded square, a blue-to-purple gradient, a stock glyph in the middle. So this round bans the container, the gradient, and the four glyphs that caused it. Every mark below is full-bleed and single-ink.

Each is a real generated PNG set, not a sketch. Judge them in the toolbar strips, not by the 128px hero: a Chrome action icon lives at 16px, and that is where icon ideas quietly fall apart.

Unquotable

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

The citation that never happens. A geometric closing double-quote, full-bleed, edge to edge: two blades, each a solid square head with a sheared tail dropping from its bottom-right corner. The first is solid. The second is the identical blade with a window punched through its head — a quotation whose second half never closes.

This is the only concept aimed at the outcome rather than the mechanism. The user's real stake is not “is my HTML server-rendered”, it is “can an AI answer quote me”. A quote is a pair; a pair missing half is a citation that will not happen.

One flat editor's-mark vermillion #DE4B2A. Its luminance (~0.21) sits between both toolbar greys, so a single ink measures 3.67:1 on light and 3.52:1 on dark — verified across all 111 opaque pixels of the 16px file.

16px: it still reads as a quotation mark. Heads and window land on whole pixels — 7×7 blocks, a 3px void, 2px walls — and only the two diagonal tails antialias, each keeping a 2–3px solid core.

Honest caveat: in isolation the hollow blade resembles a 9. The adjacent solid twin is what fixes the read, so the pairing is load-bearing — this mark cannot be used as a single blade.

Rejected: a pennant blade (read as a bookmark); guillemets (read as <>); a faded second mark (fails the silhouette test); an amputated tail (broke the pair); teal as a second ink (could not clear 3:1 on both toolbars).

Dissolve

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

Matter that stopped holding together. A solid vermilion mass fills the left third of the frame, full-bleed on three sides, and its right edge comes apart into thirteen discrete 2×2 squares that thin monotonically — 5, 3, 2, 2, 1 — and stop before reaching the far edge. Coherent matter, serrated edge, scatter, void.

The surviving fraction is literally a third of the frame, which is roughly what a client-rendered page actually delivers to a crawler. Squares rather than organic specks because the decay is raster decay — pixels dying, not smoke.

The colour travels from vermilion #E93E12 to burnt orange #D26516, but every stop is pinned near relative luminance Y=0.213 — the point that maximises worst-case contrast against both toolbars. So the hue moves and the contrast does not: 3.36–3.65:1 light, 3.53–3.84:1 dark.

16px: 148 opaque pixels, zero partial alpha — grid alignment means no antialiasing at all. The mass and the serrated edge read perfectly.

Honest caveat: below 32px the hue ramp is invisible, and the three farthest squares read as specks rather than as individual pixels.

Rejected: an ordered Bayer dither (checkerboards at 50% coverage — texture, not decay); notching the mass edge (read unmistakably as a capital E); alpha-fading the tail, and a brightness ramp (anything reading “nearly gone” on white is invisible on charcoal).

Redacted

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

Your content exists; the machine is not allowed to have it. Three brutally heavy struck-out bars run off the left edge in a descending rag — blacked-out lines of text — and from the last one a thin tail escapes past the ink to the full measure of the first line: the one sliver the crawler still gets. A small paper chip is punched through the top bar, one fragment that was not covered properly.

No tile, no plate, no gradient. The bars bleed off the left edge so the mark is a crop of a larger struck-out mass rather than an object sitting on a background. The redaction is the mark, not an annotation on a drawn document.

Colour was decided by measurement, not taste. Redaction wants to be black, and black is unusable: on Chrome's dark toolbar #292a2d a near-black bar measures ~1.3:1 and the icon simply is not there. The luminance band that clears 3:1 against both #f1f3f4 and #292a2d is narrow — relative luminance 0.17–0.28 — and only one convincing redaction colour lives inside it: the red of a classified stamp. #E8412F measures 3.61:1 light and 3.57:1 dark, deliberately balanced rather than optimised for either. That band is too narrow to hold a second ink, so the mark is one colour.

The paper chip #FFF7EC would disappear on a light toolbar if it ever touched it, so it never does — it is inset with a full grid unit of ink on all four sides. Its contrast is against the ink (3.78:1), which is identical in both themes.

16px: the 16px file contains zero partially-covered pixels — two opaque colours, every edge on a whole pixel. The rag, the escaping tail and the chip all survive. What is lost is nuance: at 16px the chip is 3×2px and reads as a notch rather than as a fragment of a word.

Honest caveat: heavy left-aligned bars of descending length sit near the align-left / list glyph. The weight is what separates them — these are 4/16 of the canvas thick, where a text glyph is nearer 1.5/16 — but the redaction reading is helped by knowing the product.

Rejected: two equal full-bleed bars (read as a flag, no ragged rhythm); near-black ink (dies on the dark toolbar — the whole reason for the red); amber or paper for the surviving fragment (1.4:1 and 1.05:1 on a light toolbar, so the punchline vanished exactly where it mattered); a full-height slot cut through a bar (reads as a gap on light and a glow on dark — two different marks); a second red for tonal depth (nothing else fits the 0.17–0.28 band).

Wildcard

northwind.example/blog light
northwind.example/blog dark

A page that is a shell — so, a film-set facade. A cornice-capped false front with a doorway you see straight through, held up from behind by a single diagonal prop, standing on a ground line.

The brief's own phrase for a client-rendered page is “a shell”, and a facade is a shell: it passes as a building from the street — ranks in Google, passes every SEO check — and has nothing behind it. The tool's whole job is walking round the back to show you the prop.

The doorway is real alpha transparency, so the browser toolbar itself shows through the front. Whatever is behind this page, it is only ever the browser.

One construction-rust family, every tone pinned inside Y ∈ [0.19, 0.22] — the only band clearing 3:1 against both toolbar greys. Measured 3.51–3.88:1 light, 3.32–3.68:1 dark on every solid pixel.

Honest caveat, and it is mine rather than the designer's: the concept is the sharpest of the four and the execution is the most ambiguous. At a glance it can read as an arch, or as a letterform with a slash. The prop is the weak element — a diagonal can never be grid-aligned, so it antialiases, and the ~2.5px daylight triangle beneath it is what carries “propped” rather than “buttressed”. That triangle is the first thing a poor downscaler would lose.

Rejected: a key blank (reads as a password manager); an empty plinth (in an SEO toolbar a podium reads as “rankings” — actively misleading); a bridge with the span missing (two blocks and a gap, says nothing); a prism (stock creative-tool mark); a bottom-open arch (read as the letter n); a round-capped strut (read as a mug handle).
